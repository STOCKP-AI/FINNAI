**jar-4. Explain the Sharpe ratio in simple words.**

> **Sharpe ratio – plain English**

The Sharpe ratio tells you how much extra return you’re getting for each unit of risk you take.  
- **Return** = the average gain (or loss) of an investment over a period.  
- **Risk** = how much the price swings around that average, measured by *volatility* (the standard deviation of returns).  

The formula is simply **return ÷ volatility**. A higher ratio means the investment has delivered more reward for the amount of risk taken, while a low or negative ratio means you’re getting little reward or even losing money for the risk you’re bearing.

Investors use it to compare different assets or portfolios: the one with the higher Sharpe ratio is usually considered more efficient. Remember, the ratio looks only at past price behaviour and doesn’t guarantee future performance. For personal advice, consult a SEBI‑registered investment adviser.
